Deck Tech: Kydele and Thrasios

September 8, 2026

The cistern contains, the fountain overflows.
          – William Blake

Introduction

I have often said that drawing cards and making mana are the two most enjoyable game actions in Magic: the Gathering. With this Commander deck, prepare to maximize them both with a lot of Simic goodstuff.

The twin commanders are Kydele, Chosen of Kruphix and Thrasios, Triton Hero.

The commander choice immediate raises the question of partners. Why pick two commanders in the same color identity? Using a different partner, like Vial Smasher the Fierce or Tymna the Weaver would unlock two extra colors.

With two colors, we can develop a much more focused deck with a more consistent mana base. It is not necessary to devote a large number of slots to color fixing. In philosophy, Kydele is the primary commander in this deck, while Thrasios is an additional value engine in the command zone. The deck produces interesting games which frequently result in victories, so I feel no desire to add more colors.

Bracketing

I consider this deck to be a powerful Bracket 3. While it has the ability to win on turn 2 with the ideal draw, it was not constructed with the intent of being as fast as possible. It lacks an optimal manabase, and many of the free spells and fast mana that would be expected of a Bracket 4 deck. The main limiting factor is the inconvenience of buying cards due to prohibitive monetary cost or just general annoyance with online retailers.

The Game Plan

K&T is a combo deck. It typically develops a very limited boardstate—interaction resides mostly in the hand—aiming literally to keep its cards close to the chest before winning the game in a brief explosive sequence.

Combos

The primary combo is Kydele, Chosen of Kruphix plus Umbral Mantle plus four cards drawn that turn, providing infinite colorless mana and infinite power. The latter is usually irrelevant, though every so often you’ll kill a player who forgot to leave up a blocker.

The more frequent win condition is Laboratory Maniac. Between Thrasios, Triton Hero himself, Enter the Infinite, and the draw-X spells Stroke of Genius1 and Mind Spring, it’s trivial to convert infinite mana into an empty library.

In times where a true infinite combo fails to materialize, dropping Omniscience on the table is usually sufficient to end the game shortly. Enter the Infinite is a similar high-mana play which ends the game promptly.

The secondary infinite combo is Isochron Scepter plus Dramatic Reversal plus any nonland mana source. Both cards are independently very good, which is ironically why I term this the secondary combo. Slamming a Scepter with Counterspell or Brainstorm can be stronger than ill-timed infinite mana, and can help guarantee a subsequent Kydele kill.

Getting More Bang for our Buck

While card draw is always beneficial, Kydele often struggles until she goes off.. Let’s say we draw three cards on a given turn, e.g. with a couple cantrips. Kydele will thus be a four-mana dork who taps for three—not nothing, but also not great. There will often be a period before the combo turn where you’ll need to lay low and be interactive, and gain incremental value before you find the missing piece or reach a critical mass.

To smooth out this awkward period, there are a number of ways to get more value out of Kydele and card draw in general. Untappers like Kiora's Follower and Dramatic Reversal double her output, while the draw triggers on Horizon Chimera and Chasm Skulker generate some nice value against aggressive decks.

Note the inclusion of Magus of the Candelabra. This card is deceptively effective—laundering colorless mana into colors is very useful. With enough mana, it can also unlock tapped lands generated by Thrasios or ramp spells like Cultivate.

Mistcutter Hydra and Genesis Hydra can convert big mana into big damage, but they are also versatile enough to be played in the midgame. Protection from Blue allows the former to apply pressure to a good portion of opponents, while the latter can help find a missing combo piece while also being a body on the board.

Regrowth is incredibly useful as a way to re-use spells in both the early and midgame, or to recover a combo from removal. It also goes great under an Isochron Scepter.

The overall mana curve is relatively low—mostly 2s and 3s—though a number of more expensive cards can prove backbreaking for your opponents if they fail to deal with them. -

  • Kruphix, God of Horizons is hard to remove and lets you snowball your Kydele mana to play around countermagic.
  • Psychosis Crawler pings opponents on each draw, which can easily become a good chunk of damage in grindier games.
  • Arcanis the Omnipotent and Consecrated Sphinx are both incredibly strong card draw engines. Untapping with either one usually results in an imminent win. The Sphinx is strong even if it doesn’t survive a full turn rotation. Arcanis usually wants Lightning Greaves for haste.

Tutors

This deck eschews traditional tutors in favor of more card draw, with Mystical Tutor as the only “pure” example of such effects2. Tutors are not as essential when we plan on drawing half our library.

That being said, this is still a combo deck, so we want to increase the probability of accessing key cards, despite the Simic color identity lacking cheap general tutors. Blue can tutor for artifact combo pieces, but something like Fabricate is too constrained. Tutors should be good even when not fetching combo pieces. Green’s creature tutors are mostly irrelevant.

A solution manifests in the transmute cards from original Ravnica. These are cards that can tutor based on mana value, which is very useful for a low-curve deck where cards are clustered around two and three:

  • Muddle the Mixture is a counterspell that can grab Isochron Scepter or Dramatic Reversal, or a Lightning Greaves to protect Kydele.
  • Drift of Phantasms is a blocker that can grab Umbral Mantle or Laboratory Maniac.

Keep the transmuters in mind when looking at later sections—we can get away with running fewer instances of certain effects, since there is effectively an extra copy of each two-or-three-drop.

Card Draw

Card draw spells are the backbone of this deck. Even if they are not being used to immediately combo off, card advantage is good. We run the full suite of one-mana cantrips, given here in order of how much I like to see them in my opening hand:

  • Brainstorm
  • Preordain
  • Ponder
  • Serum Visions
  • Opt

With Kydele out, all of these are essentially free (Brainstorm basically becomes a Dark Ritual).

Some card draw spells don’t have an immediate payoff, in favor of drawing cards over future turns. Howling Mine is the prototype; Dictate of Kruphix is slightly better in that you can draw the first card. Jace Beleren is the most flexible, but is vulnerable to removal. The tradeoff is letting the opponents draw cards. In a four-player game, Howling Mine gives away three cards for every one you draw. This tradeoff is offset by the fact that we get more value from each draw, since we get cards and mana, but caution is necessary. In many situations, it is best not to give the other players anything for free.

The aforementioned draw-X spells are useful in two cases. Before the combo turn, they can double Kydele’s output. An especially powerful pattern is <draw 2> → Tap Kydele → Mind Spring → Untap Kydele with Kiora's Follower.

Stroke of Genius and Mind Spring are the classical examples. Minds Aglow is a personal favorite. At worst it is sorcery-speed Prosperity, at best the other players subsidize your combo turn (Just be warned that this only tends to work once per playgroup…). A caveat with this card is that if you aren’t careful, your opponents may be able to generate enough mana to mill you out.

Other miscellaneous draw spells include Mystic Remora for free cards early in the game, Compulsive Research as an efficient way to draw three cards at once, instantly reaching critical mass with Kydele, and Frantic Search as a card-filtering mana generator.

Alhammaret's Archive is an expensive artifact that doesn’t do anything when it comes down, but can very quickly get out of hand. It makes each of these draw spells mana-positive.

Special attention must be paid to Jace's Archivist, A.K.A. Windfall on a stick. Untapping with this creature and Kydele can easily win the game—usually at least one player will have 5+ cards in hand. Adding an untapper or Archive into the mix is usually backbreaking.

Interaction

This deck maintains a limited battlefield presence, so it needs to be extra interactive both to protect its own gameplan and to keep opponents from building up too efficiently. Counterspell, Spell Pierce, Swan Song, Arcane Denial, Disallow, and Cryptic Command make up the counterspell suite. Arcane Denial is better in this deck than others, as the delayed card draw can be used to set up a nice Kydele turn.

For on-board interaction in Simic, I’m a big fan of so-called “swap removal”, where an opponent gets a token in exchange for a destroyed creature. Pongify and Rapid Hybridization are among the most efficient creature removal in Blue, while Beast Within is effective against any permanent. The tradeoff of an opposing 3/3 is mostly irrelevant in a game with 40 life.

Curse of the Swine puts this effect on an X-cost exile boardwipe allowing for surgical removal of a number of problematic creatures. Of course, Cyclonic Rift remains the best boardwipe in the colors. Remember that it can be used as a two-mana bounce spell, then Regrowthed back when needed. Note that both spells have a mana value of two, letting Muddle the Mixture tutor for them.

Broken Bond3 and Manglehorn are effective against artifacts, though they may be swapped out with other similar spells. I recommend replacements with the same mana values, so the transmutors can find them.

Lastly, I have been experimenting with Propaganda to hedge against go-wide strategies. I have yet to see how effective it is.

Note that the interaction suite should be tailored to one’s playgroup—I don’t run any graveyard hate because none of my friends played graveyard strategies, for example.

Ramp

The critical moment for this deck usually occurs on turn four or five. As such, we only have room for a couple of ramp spells to get there faster. Once Kydele is down, she will provide mana acceleration.

We run the classics: the obvious Sol Ring, with Cultivate, and Rampant Growth for ramp and color fixing. Explore is extra useful in this deck, remaining useful into the late game as it grants an extra mana to Kydele. Thought Vessel and Commander's Sphere are the other mana rocks. The former is mostly for convenience, as nobody wants to sit there while you discard from a 20 card hand. The latter is useful as a free card draw (+1 mana with Kydele), though it is a little slow.

Lastly, Burgeoning can put you very far ahead if you are able to refill your hand fast enough. I’m not convinced that the expected value is higher than something else to fill the slot, but even a weaker Burgeoning will usually net at least one or two extra land drops.

Mana Base

The mana base consists of 39 assorted Simic lands. There are a few callouts:

  • Misty Rainforest is of course a combo with Brainstorm.
  • Simic Growth Chamber is usually a bad card, but here it combos with Frantic Search, Kiora's Follower, and Magus of the Candelabra.
  • Lonely Sandbar is a cantrip if necessary.
  • Lumbering Falls can serve as a blocker in an emergency.
